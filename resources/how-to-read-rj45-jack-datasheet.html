How to Read an RJ45 Jack Datasheet: Every Spec That Matters

Updated July 16, 2026 · By VOOHU Electronics

An RJ45 jack — the female modular socket (母座) the cable plug mates into — hides a surprising amount of engineering behind a short datasheet. Choose the wrong line and the port fails EMC, cooks under PoE, will not fit the enclosure, or drops link at 2.5G. This guide reads the RJ45 jack datasheet the way a hardware engineer does: what each spec means, the value to expect, and the standard behind it — with real options drawn from VOOHU's jack range and parameters sourced from IEC 60603-7, ANSI/TIA-568 and IEEE 802.3.

Short answer: Read eight lines first — data rate/category, integrated magnetics vs discrete, PoE current rating, mount (THT/SMT), tab orientation, shielding (UTP/STP), operating temperature, and durability (≥750 mating cycles, IEC 60603-7). Those fix speed, power, board fit and field life. The remaining electrical lines — contact resistance, insulation resistance, dielectric withstanding voltage and (on a magjack) isolation — confirm build quality. Remember: the mount and mechanical form are physical choices; the contact system and category set the electrical class.

Mechanical & format lines — the ones that decide fit

These lines rarely change the electrical class, but get one wrong and the board comes back. Ports/gang (1×N, 2×N) drive board utilization and pin-header coplanarity — larger 2×N modules are more prone to reflow warping, so look for positioning posts. Tab orientation (up/down) decides whether the jack mounts on the front or back of the PCB and which way the shell window faces. Body angle — 45°, 90° right-angle or 180° vertical — plus offset/sink-type and low-profile variants set the Z-height; sink-type jacks drop into a board cut-out to shrink the enclosure but consume edge routing. Mount is DIP (through-hole, strongest against plug-in force) or SMT (automation-friendly, add board locks). See our companion guides on THT vs SMT mounting and tab-up vs tab-down orientation for the full trade-offs.

Electrical & transmission lines — the ones that decide performance

The data rate / category is the headline: an 8P8C jack is qualified as a component to a TIA category by meeting the connector NEXT (near-end crosstalk) and return-loss limits at that category's bandwidth. The channel you build is limited by the weakest link, so a Cat6A jack only yields a Cat6A channel with Cat6A cable.

The PoE current rating is the most misread line. Under Power over Ethernet the same contacts carry data and DC power, so the jack must be rated for the current, not just the speed. VOOHU exposes discrete steps from 350 mA up to 1.5 A per contact; for IEEE 802.3bt (PoE++, up to 90 W across all four pairs) pick an integrated magjack rated for roughly ≥1 A per pair and check the terminal temperature rise. On an integrated magjack, also read the isolation (Hi-Pot ≥1500 V rms) — the transformer's primary-to-secondary barrier — which is a different figure from the connector's own dielectric withstanding voltage (1000 V AC). Finally, contact resistance (≤20 mΩ) and insulation resistance (≥500 MΩ) are the quiet quality lines; they, together with gold plating thickness, predict how the contact holds up over its ≥750-cycle life.

Read a datasheet in 60 seconds

  • Speed & power: confirm the data rate (e.g. 100/1000M or 10GBASE-T) and, if the port carries PoE, the per-contact current (up to 1.5 A). These are independent lines.
  • Magnetics: integrated (magjack, saves ~60–120 mm² of board and shortens traces) or discrete (flexible layout, easier repair, better PoE heat handling)? See integrated vs discrete.
  • Form & fit: ports/gang, tab up/down, angle (45/90/180°), offset/low-profile, and DIP vs SMT — do they fit the enclosure and the assembly line?
  • Environment: shielding (UTP/STP) and operating temperature (0–70 °C commercial vs −40 to +85/+105 °C industrial) matched to where the box lives.
  • Quality & life: durability (≥750 cycles), plating tier, contact/insulation resistance, DWV, and magjack isolation (≥1500 V rms).

Common mistakes when reading the spec sheet

  • Reading speed but not PoE current. A Gigabit jack can be non-PoE; if the design is 802.3bt, a jack under-rated for current will overheat at the contacts.
  • Confusing DWV with magjack isolation. 1000 V AC (connector insulation) and ≥1500 V rms (transformer Hi-Pot) are separate lines that test different barriers.
  • Assuming the jack alone sets the category. The channel is limited by the weaker of jack and cable; match categories end to end.
  • Ignoring temperature grade. A 0–70 °C commercial jack in an outdoor or cabinet enclosure is a field return; specify −40 to +85 °C where needed.
  • Overlooking the mount vs process match. Reflowing a wave-only part, or trusting SMT pads with no board locks, cracks joints after a few hundred plug cycles.
  • Skipping plating for cost. Thin gold (6–15 µin) is fine indoors, but high-cycle or corrosive service needs 30–50 µin — the datasheet plating line tells you which.

Frequently asked questions

Which specs on an RJ45 jack datasheet matter most?

Read eight lines first: data rate / category (10/100M up to 10GBASE-T), whether the magnetics are integrated (magjack) or discrete, PoE current rating per contact (up to 1.5 A), mount (through-hole DIP or SMT), tab orientation (up or down), shielding (UTP or STP), operating temperature (commercial 0 to +70 °C vs industrial −40 to +85/+105 °C), and durability (≥750 mating cycles per IEC 60603-7). Together they fix the port's speed, power, board fit and field life; the remaining electrical lines confirm quality.

What does the PoE current rating on an RJ45 jack mean?

It is the maximum continuous DC current each contact can carry without overheating, because under Power over Ethernet the same pins carry both data and power. VOOHU lists jack options from 350 mA up to 1.5 A per contact. For IEEE 802.3bt (PoE++, up to 90 W across all four pairs) choose an integrated magjack rated for at least about 1 A per pair and verify the terminal temperature rise. Data rate and PoE current are independent lines, so a Gigabit jack can be non-PoE or rated to 1.5 A.

What is the difference between a jack's dielectric withstanding voltage and a magjack's isolation voltage?

Dielectric withstanding voltage (DWV), commonly 1000 V AC for one minute on IEC 60603-7-class datasheets, tests the connector's own insulation between contacts and shell. A magjack's isolation voltage (Hi-Pot, ≥1500 V rms per IEEE 802.3) tests the built-in transformer's primary-to-secondary barrier. A discrete RJ45 jack lists only DWV; an integrated-magnetics jack lists both, so do not confuse the two figures.

Does an RJ45 jack's Cat5e/Cat6/Cat6A rating come from the datasheet or the cable?

Both the jack and the cable carry a category rating, and the channel is limited by the weaker of the two. The jack datasheet states the component category it was qualified to under ANSI/TIA-568 by meeting the connector NEXT and return-loss limits: Cat5e to 100 MHz, Cat6 to 250 MHz and Cat6A to 500 MHz. Pair a Cat6A jack with Cat6A cable to actually obtain a Cat6A channel.
