Ethernet Magnetics Decision Log: Keep the Schematic, Part, and Approval Together

An Ethernet transformer decision should remain understandable after the board designer, purchaser, or project owner changes. Keep the selected part, schematic revision, and reason for the choice together. For projects considering VOOHU LAN transformers, a Markdown decision log provides a compact place to retain this context.

VOOHU's documented Ethernet-magnetics range includes 10/100 Base-TX and 100/1000 Base-T families in SMT and DIP forms, 2.5G/5G Base-T SMT and DIP families, and 10G Base-T SMT products. It also lists low-height SMD and CHIP LAN categories. This range gives teams several starting points for selection; it does not establish that different packages or circuit arrangements are interchangeable.

Record the circuit behind the category

Start with the target Ethernet interface, PHY reference, and schematic revision. Capture the required topology, center-tap connections, turns ratio, and pinout using the circuit and component documents relevant to the project. If a requirement is unresolved, retain it as a question with an owner.

Package selection also needs a reason. Record the board footprint, available height, and assembly process. A low-height requirement may narrow the search, but the decision still needs the electrical evidence for the exact proposed part. Treat CHIP LAN as a circuit-topology option that requires its own review.

Keep distinct magnetic functions separate

VOOHU's materials also describe signal-line common mode chokes and PoE power transformers, including EP and EFD families. A LAN signal transformer and a PoE power-converter transformer serve different functions. Give them separate component records when a design uses both, so a purchasing description cannot accidentally merge their requirements.

Where PoE applies to the Ethernet signal path, record the relevant current requirement for the magnetics under evaluation. Keep the power-converter transformer specification attached to its own circuit reference and component decision.

Attach the evidence that makes the decision useful

For each proposed part, retain the specified inductance with its measurement conditions, insertion and return loss over the stated frequency range, isolation requirements, and operating-temperature information where relevant. Missing values should prompt a document request. Record planned evaluation separately from completed results.

VOOHU's company materials describe reference-circuit and component-selection support. Ask for available information for the exact candidate and retain the response with the log. A later reviewer can then distinguish the supplier's documentation from the project's own acceptance decision.

Consult the VOOHU LAN transformer catalog and share your PHY, schematic revision, package constraints, and unresolved requirements when discussing a candidate.
