8P8C vs RJ45: Are They the Same Connector? Jack Terminology Explained

Datasheets say “8P8C,” catalogs say “RJ45,” and installers say “the network jack.” Are they the same thing? This VOOHU Electronics guide clears up the 8P8C-versus-RJ45 confusion, explains the RJ45S history behind the name, and shows what actually matters when you specify an Ethernet jack — the female modular socket (母座) your cable plugs into.

The short answer

For any practical purpose, 8P8C and RJ45 describe the same connector: the 8-position, 8-contact modular jack used for Ethernet. 8P8C (“8 Position 8 Contact”) is the precise engineering name, standardized in IEC 60603-7. RJ45 is the common name the networking industry uses for that jack. The only nuance is historical: “RJ45” originally referred to a telephone wiring registration, not the Ethernet connector — but the label stuck, and today the two terms are used interchangeably. When you buy an “RJ45 jack” from VOOHU, you are buying an 8P8C modular socket; VOOHU's own product page describes it as an “8P8C network jack.”

Why “RJ45” is technically the wrong name (but everyone uses it)

“RJ” stands for Registered Jack, a series of standardized telephone interfaces defined under the US Universal Service Order Codes (USOC). An RJ code specifies wiring and function, not just a connector shape. The original RJ45S was a keyed, single-line data jack that used a programming resistor — it had a little plastic key that the ordinary 8P8C Ethernet plug does not, so an Ethernet plug would not even seat in a true RJ45S.

When structured-cabling systems adopted the 8-position modular jack in the 1980s–1990s, the “RJ45” label came along informally and never left. So the connector on your switch, wall plate, or PCB is really an 8P8C modular jack; “RJ45” is the name we all say. This is why careful datasheets and standards documents write 8P8C, while marketing, retail, and installers write RJ45. Both point to the same part.

Rule of thumb. If a document says 8P8C, RJ45, or “network jack” for an Ethernet port, it means the same 8-position modular socket. The name never tells you the category, shielding, or whether magnetics are built in — those must be specified separately.

What actually differs from jack to jack

Since the 8P8C-versus-RJ45 distinction is only a naming one, here is what genuinely separates one Ethernet jack from another — the things worth putting on your BOM:

Category & bandwidth

A Cat5e 8P8C jack and a Cat6A 8P8C jack share the same 8-position body but are engineered for different crosstalk and return-loss limits. Match the jack category to the link speed — see our Cat5e vs Cat6 vs Cat6A jack guide.

Shielding

Unshielded (UTP) versus shielded (FTP/STP) 8P8C jacks change EMC behavior and grounding. VOOHU offers shield-tab and EMI-gasket options on its RJ45 range.

Contact plating

Gold thickness over a nickel underplate sets durability and contact resistance. Higher tiers (up to 50 µin) resist wear on high-insertion ports — our materials & plating guide covers the tiers.

Discrete vs integrated magnetics

Every Ethernet 8P8C port needs isolation magnetics. You can use a plain jack plus a separate LAN transformer, or an all-in-one integrated-magnetics magjack. VOOHU's SYT-series magjacks integrate the transformer into the 8P8C housing at 350 µH OCL, 1CT:1CT turns ratio, 2250 V DC isolation, insertion loss ≤1.0 dB and 750 mating cycles, with built-in support up to 10G BASE-T and HDBaseT.

Common mistakes

The 8P8C/RJ45 mix-up is harmless by itself, but the assumptions around it cause real errors:

  • Thinking “RJ45” guarantees a category. The name says nothing about Cat5e vs Cat6A. Always specify the category and bandwidth you need.
  • Assuming every 8-position jack is keyed like RJ45S. Standard Ethernet 8P8C jacks are un-keyed; the historical RJ45S key is not present on normal network jacks.
  • Confusing 8P8C with 8P4C or 6P. A jack can have 8 positions but fewer loaded contacts. For Gigabit and PoE you need all eight contacts populated.
  • Forgetting magnetics. An 8P8C jack alone is not an Ethernet front end — add a discrete transformer or use an integrated magjack.
  • Ignoring plating and mating-cycle rating. Field ports that are plugged often need higher gold tiers to stay within the 750-cycle durability margin. See mating cycles & durability.

Frequently asked questions

Is 8P8C the same as RJ45?
In everyday use, yes. 8P8C (8 Position 8 Contact) is the precise technical name for the modular jack standardized in IEC 60603-7; RJ45 is the common industry name for that same jack when it carries Ethernet. The socket you plug an Ethernet cable into is an 8P8C modular jack. Strictly, “RJ45” is an old telephone wiring registration, but the industry now uses the two terms interchangeably.
Why is the Ethernet connector called RJ45 if that is technically wrong?
RJ45 comes from the US Registered Jack system (USOC). The original RJ45S was a keyed single-line telephone jack with a programming resistor, not the Ethernet connector. Because it also used an 8-position modular body, the RJ45 label transferred to the 8P8C jack when structured cabling adopted it, and it stuck — even though the correct engineering term is 8P8C per IEC 60603-7.
Is there any real difference between an 8P8C jack and an RJ45 jack?
No electrical or mechanical difference for Ethernet: both names describe the same 8-position, 8-contact modular socket on a 1.02 mm contact pitch. What varies between jacks is category and bandwidth (Cat5e to Cat8), shielding, contact plating, mounting (through-hole or SMT), and whether isolation magnetics are integrated — specify those, not the name.
How many mating cycles does an 8P8C RJ45 jack last?
IEC 60603-7 sets a minimum durability of 750 mating cycles for the 8P8C modular jack. VOOHU RJ45 and integrated-magnetics magjack sockets are built to meet this 750-cycle rating, with higher gold-plating tiers such as 50 µin available for ports that are plugged frequently.
