RJ45 Jack Soldering & Moisture Sensitivity (MSL): Reflow, Wave & Hand-Soldering Guide

Updated July 20, 2026 · By VOOHU Electronics

An RJ45 jack (the female modular socket, or 母座) looks like a rugged mechanical part, but the way you solder it decides whether it survives assembly — especially an integrated-magnetics magjack socket, which hides a transformer and fine magnet wire inside the housing. This guide explains how to solder RJ45 jacks correctly: the lead-free reflow profile, the wave and hand-soldering windows, and the JEDEC moisture-sensitivity-level (MSL) rules that stop SMT sockets from “popcorning” on the line.

Short answer: Solder a plain through-hole (DIP) RJ45 jack by wave or selective soldering at roughly 250–260°C for 3–5 s. Solder an SMT integrated-magnetics jack with a single lead-free reflow pass to the IPC/JEDEC J-STD-020 profile — ~217°C liquidus, 60–150 s above liquidus, 260°C peak maximum — and respect its MSL floor life (bake per J-STD-033 if the dry-pack bag has been open too long). Always confirm the exact peak temperature, pass count and MSL rating on the specific part datasheet.

Why RJ45 jacks are more heat- and moisture-sensitive than they look

A bare unshielded or shielded RJ45 jack is mostly a thermoplastic housing, a metal shield and eight phosphor-bronze contacts — robust, and forgiving of heat. An integrated-magnetics RJ45 jack (magjack) is a different animal: it packs an Ethernet transformer, sometimes a common-mode choke, and enamelled magnet wire into the same body that mates with the plug. That internal assembly introduces two failure modes that plain jacks do not have:

  • Thermal damage — excess peak temperature or dwell can soften or warp the housing, relax the contact spring, or degrade the enamel on the internal magnet wire, shifting the magnetics’ performance.
  • Moisture / popcorning — the plastic and the winding varnish absorb ambient humidity. If a moisture-laden part is reflowed, trapped water flashes to steam and cracks or delaminates the body — the classic “popcorn” effect. This is why SMT magjacks carry a moisture-sensitivity level.

The RJ45 mating interface itself is defined by IEC 60603-7; category transmission performance follows ANSI/TIA-568. Neither standard tells you how to solder the jack — that is governed by the soldering standards below, plus the connector’s own datasheet.

Lead-free reflow profile for SMT RJ45 jacks (per J-STD-020)

SMT integrated-magnetics jacks are classified for reflow using IPC/JEDEC J-STD-020. Treat the numbers below as the outer envelope for a single SnAgCu (lead-free) pass; your paste and the part datasheet may specify a tighter window.

Key discipline: keep the peak under the datasheet limit and the time near peak short. A magjack that sees two uncontrolled reflow passes (for example a double-sided board where the jack is reflowed twice) is a common, avoidable cause of shifted return-loss and intermittent link-up.

Moisture Sensitivity Level (MSL): floor life and baking

MSL, defined in IPC/JEDEC J-STD-020 and handled per IPC/JEDEC J-STD-033, tells you how long an SMT jack may sit out of its sealed moisture-barrier bag (MBB) before it must be reflowed — or re-baked. The clock starts the moment the bag is opened. Check the humidity indicator card (HIC) inside the bag; if it shows the part has taken on moisture, bake before use regardless of the timer.

Integrated-magnetics RJ45 jacks are typically supplied around MSL 2 to MSL 4. If the floor life is exceeded, restore the part with a bake (commonly 125°C, duration per J-STD-033 for the package and MSL) and reset the clock. Plain through-hole jacks without magnetics are generally not moisture-sensitive in the same way, but should still be kept dry and clean for good solderability.

Wave & selective soldering (through-hole jacks)

Typical practice for DIP RJ45 jacks: topside preheat ~100–130°C, solder pot ~250–260°C, contact time 3–5 s, single pass. Selective soldering with a local nozzle is ideal for a jack next to heat-sensitive neighbours because it confines the heat to the jack’s pins.

Hand soldering & rework

For touch-up and prototypes, use a temperature-controlled iron around 350–370°C, 3–5 s per contact, and adequate tip mass for the large ground/shield tabs (an under-powered iron makes cold joints; camping the tip too long melts the housing). Follow IPC-7711/7721 for rework of magnetics-bearing parts.

Common RJ45-jack soldering mistakes

  • Treating an SMT magjack like a passive. Skipping the MSL bake after the bag’s floor life expired is the number-one cause of popcorned, cracked sockets.
  • Ignoring the HIC. The humidity indicator card overrides the timer — a pink card means bake, even if the part was “only opened yesterday.”
  • A second, unqualified reflow pass. Double-reflowing a magjack without checking the datasheet shifts the magnetics and can crack the body.
  • Over-long wave contact. Cooking a through-hole magjack in the pot degrades the internal windings even when the joints look perfect.
  • Cold joints on shield/ground tabs. Large thermal mass with a small iron leaves weak grounds — then the port fails EMC or ESD later.
  • Wrong stencil/paste on fine SMT tabs. Insufficient paste volume on the shield legs causes tombstoning or opens.

Frequently asked questions

Can you reflow-solder an RJ45 magjack?

Yes. SMT integrated-magnetics RJ45 jacks are designed for one lead-free reflow pass following the JEDEC J-STD-020 profile, peak 260°C maximum. Confirm the exact peak and pass count on the part datasheet, and follow the MSL floor-life/bake rules so the internal magnetics and housing are not damaged.

What MSL do integrated-magnetics RJ45 jacks have?

It varies by part and is printed on the dry-pack label and datasheet — commonly MSL 2 to MSL 4 (roughly one year down to 72 hours of floor life). If the floor life is exceeded, bake per J-STD-033 before reflow to avoid popcorning.

Can through-hole (DIP) RJ45 jacks be wave-soldered?

Yes. DIP RJ45 jacks are wave- and selective-solder friendly — typically ~250–260°C pot, 3–5 s, single pass. VOOHU offers both DIP and SMT jacks, so match the process to the part.

Why does an RJ45 jack fail right after soldering?

Usually heat or moisture damage: popcorning from a skipped MSL bake, a warped/cracked housing from too high a peak, degraded internal magnetics, or cold joints on the shield tabs. Controlling the profile and MSL handling prevents nearly all of these.
